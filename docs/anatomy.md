# Anatomy in My Little Brain

The brain uses reconstructed public human template geometry. Its cortical folds are the **fsaverage pial surfaces at 41k vertices per hemisphere**, distributed through neuromaps. The internal structures are the **ENIGMA Toolbox subcortical template surfaces**: left and right accumbens, amygdala, caudate, hippocampus, pallidum, putamen, thalamus, and lateral ventricles. Ventricles are fluid spaces, not nuclei.

The public fsaverage `aseg.mgz` segmentation adds both cerebellar hemispheres, the brainstem (including pons and medulla), ventral diencephalon, cerebral white matter, corpus callosum, and third and fourth ventricles. These fill structures absent from the ENIGMA nucleus-only surfaces. White matter is faintly translucent in Inside mode so it provides anatomical context while leaving the nuclei visible. The cerebellum and brainstem remain visible in normal mode. Labels are reconstructed with marching cubes after 0.65-voxel Gaussian boundary smoothing and aligned using the volume’s tkregister transform. Segmentation resolution cannot reproduce fine cerebellar folia or separately identify every small brainstem nucleus.

The cortex contains 163,840 triangles; the internal structures contain 102,836. The additional segmentation meshes contain 522,730 triangles. All 28 meshes together contain 789,406 triangles and are 15,782,568 bytes before HTTP compression. They retain the published 41k cortical resolution and the subcortical topology without further decimation. The lighter published cortical mesh avoids shipping the full 164k-per-hemisphere template while preserving actual folds. Sulcal-depth values provide restrained vertex shading; they are anatomical shape values, not activity or thought intensity.

The source repositories were inspected read-only. `win/data` resolves to the local public asset store; its fsaverage and ENIGMA files were converted by `scripts/convert-anatomy.py`. `IBM-1` inventories these same template sources. The scatterBrains subset contains individual scalp surfaces, not cortex, so it was not used. No participant-specific subject files, functional recordings or private anatomy are included.

These are **modified visualization assets**, not the original distribution files. The manifest records each original file's SHA-256, each output's SHA-256, counts, source paths and links. The conversion changes the coordinate frame and file format, splits the ENIGMA mesh using its documented vertex ordering, and adds display shading. It does not invent cortical folds or nuclei. To reproduce with Python, numpy, nibabel, scipy and scikit-image installed (the assets directory’s parent must also contain the public MNE fsaverage template):

```sh
python scripts/convert-anatomy.py --assets /path/to/win/data/assets
```

The FreeSurfer license is separately included from the official upstream `LICENSE.txt`. Rendering uses a right-handed rotation from input RAS millimetres to scene coordinates: `[-R, S - 15, A + 17] * 0.015 + [0, 0.35, 0]`. The viewer sees the anterior surface; anatomical right appears on the viewer's left. All structures use this same transform. The two source templates share a FreeSurfer-style display frame; their combination is a template visualization, not an individual registration or a validated atlas fusion.

`BrainScene` accepts `anatomyMode?: boolean`. Default mode shows opaque cortex; anatomy mode makes it translucent and shows the internal structures with distinct display colors. Loading failures show an explicit retry state instead of substituting procedural cortex. Requests are aborted on unmount, and GPU geometry and materials are disposed.

The eyeballs and optic nerve attachments remain stylized animated character features. The cervical cord is a tapered, gently curved display mesh aligned to the actual medulla, with paired ventral/dorsal rootlet fans and four cervical vertebral arches. This replaces the previous disconnected decorative stem. The cord, rootlets, and vertebrae remain stylized, not atlas geometry; the spinal cord continues within the vertebral canal and does not become bone. They are not derived from these cortical/subcortical templates. Eye motion visualizes conversational attention. Nothing in the scene depicts measured neural activity, model reasoning, a user's own anatomy, or a clinically validated anatomical model.

Drag the scene with a mouse or touch to orbit around the full anatomy. Focus the scene and use the arrow keys to orbit or Home to face forward. Four seconds after the last orbit input, the view eases to its anterior pose; reduced motion returns without animation. Flies, butterflies and floating motes are illustrative distractions, with gaze and eyelid openness modulated by simulated focus energy. They are not measures of neurological function.
